# Question bank

Pick four to six. Adapt the wording to what research found — a question that
names a specific trigger lands, a generic one signals you did no homework.

## First discovery call

- What made you look at this now rather than six months ago?
- Walk me through how this works today, start to finish.
- Who touches this process besides you?
- What have you already tried?
- What happens if nothing changes this quarter?
- How would you know in ninety days that this worked?

## Demo or evaluation call

- Which part of what I just showed matters most to you, and which part is noise?
- Who else needs to see this before it goes anywhere?
- What would make this fail inside your team?
- What are you comparing us against, including doing nothing?
- What does your process look like from here to a decision?

## Late stage, buying signals present

- What is the approval path, and who has signed off on something this size before?
- What is the start date you are working back from?
- What has stalled a purchase like this for you in the past?
- Is there a budget cycle or renewal this has to fit inside?

## Renewal or expansion

- What has this actually delivered since you bought it?
- Who uses it weekly now versus at launch?
- What changed on your side this year that we should know about?
- Where is the next version of this problem showing up for you?

## Win-back or re-engagement

- What was going on when this went quiet?
- Has the problem changed since we last spoke, or just the priority?
- Who owns this now?

## What to avoid

- Questions the brief already answers. Asking "so what does Northwind do?" after
  they published a Series B announcement wastes the goodwill research bought.
- Stacked questions. Ask one, then stop talking.
- Leading questions that pitch. "Wouldn't it help if…" is a statement wearing a
  question mark, and buyers hear it.
- Questions built on a fact nothing established. If it is in "Not established",
  it cannot be a premise — ask about it instead of assuming it.
